PURSLANE · PORTULACA OLERACEA

Purslane: One of the Richest Plant Sources of Omega-3 Fatty Acids

Purslane is unusual among leafy greens for its high content of α-linolenic acid (an omega-3 fatty acid).

A 1992 study in the Journal of the American College of Nutrition quantified its omega-3, vitamin E, vitamin C, and glutathione levels and compared them favorably to spinach. Comprehensive reviews published in 2024 reaffirm that purslane ranks among the highest terrestrial plant sources of omega-3s and contains a range of antioxidants and phytochemicals.

Nutritional highlights

  • Significant α-linolenic acid content
  • Favorable omega-6 to omega-3 ratio in many analyses
  • Additional vitamins, minerals, and antioxidant compounds

Research context

Most health-related findings remain preclinical (antioxidant, anti-inflammatory models). Human clinical trials specifically on purslane supplementation are limited.

Is purslane a replacement for fish oil?

No. It provides plant-based omega-3 (ALA), which the body converts inefficiently to EPA and DHA.
